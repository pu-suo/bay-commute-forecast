How accurate is this?

Every number on this page is measured on data the model never trained on, in the units the forecast is published in.

On the nine named commutes

Travel time in minutes, composed from detector-level forecasts and compared against what those corridors actually did. The baseline is the seasonal profile — the same weekday and time of day, averaged over history — which is a strong opponent at a day-ahead horizon and the thing any model has to beat to earn its place.

At detector level

Speed in mph, per five-minute detector reading. Split two ways: stations the model trained on, and stations it has never seen. The second is the one that matters — the network has 2,291 detectors and the model is trained on a fraction of them, so the whole design rests on the gap between these two columns being small.

What is not measured here

Surface-street time is an estimate, produced by spreading nearby freeway conditions onto local roads with a damping factor that was chosen, not fitted. There is no public source of Bay Area arterial speeds to score it against. It is labelled wherever it appears, reported separately in every route breakdown, and excluded from every figure on this page.

Roadwork is collected nightly but is not yet a model feature: Caltrans publishes current state only, so the archive began the day collection started and is still too short to train on.
